# October 2025
2025-10-31

The Scariest Month of My Life and The Importance of Security.

I've been planning to write a blog for a while. Actually, I attempted to write a progress documentation back in 2023, but that just turned into a confession to one of my classmates everyone could see.

Luckily, I no longer have classmates to confess to.

I'm inspired by one of my friends who blogs a month of his life, a format I will be directly ripping off.

A lot of changes happened in October 2025, a lot are exciting, some are horrific, but all of them are *scary*.

I graduated in June 2025, started applying in August, applied to dozens of companies, went through interviews that broke my heart, got two job offers, rejected one, accepted the other, and on October 1, 2025, I started my first job. I officially have the job title of Application Developer, I officially have coworkers, I officially have a boss, and I officially have a salary. It's a lot to process.

Right away, I got assigned a project, which made my heart explode. I went to all my interviews feigning confidence that I could do anything; I could develop apps and websites, create dashboards, make high-quality games, be an accountant, be a customer service person, develop AI models, etc. I still believe this to be true, but diving in head-first to a new role without any lifeguards surely made my muscles cramp. 

My flaws and bad habits got shone a spotlight, my weaknesses left bare naked for the world (my coworkers) to see while I fiercely hit them with a bat of discipline and getting smacked back on the face.

Slowly, I've been learning more and more. Now, I can use React and edit details without having to prompt Claude for it. I can configure security for the website I'm developing (with the help of ChatGPT), and many more.

I've always been a vibe coder. Ever since I learned about ChatGPT, I've been using it to do everything. I literally graduated because I ran my thesis through ChatGPT. But one important skill I've always known I would need with vibe coding is debugging. Especially in the early days, LLMs, even if it's Claude, tends to breakdown and cry. Using the right words to prompt the right response and being able to manually check for things and explaining them to the LLM clearly is necessary.

Claude is the best coding tool for total idiots who picked up a full stack of React-Vite-Node-TypeScript stack without knowing what any of those are. With clear prompts, it's helping me keep my job. But we have to admit that it's stupidly expensive.

I live in the Philippines, a third-world country. My salary sans taxes means paying for Claude Pro takes off 4.7%, 5.2% with taxes. Because of this, I developed a system: I have FIVE EMAIL ACCOUNTS and I used each of them at a time for Claude Free. I'll solve a problem, then my session will end, so I'll go to another account and put all the files in there again, then I'll ask the next problem until I hit the session limit. 

That worked for a while, but then it was as if I hit a giant boulder while using my electric golfcart and it crushed me. 

When I pushed my project to a different environment, it took me almost a whole week to get it to work properly. I was pulling my hair out, I felt like I was shaking in my seat, my face was falling off my head. It wasn't a fun time.

The AI-generated code that were made in different Claude accounts had their routing hardcoded into the files, making my efforts to change the IP address and their ports moot. I didn't realize this until I read the code because the whole time I was just sending every file I can think of to Claude. Once I figured out (manually) what had happened, I fixed it relatively quickly and I felt better, but it was still a massive waste. I spent all day crying and restarting the environment when I could have just read the code and fixed it myself.

Now, I'm paying for Claude Code. Even though each month costs two days of work, at least I can finish two days of work in a day. Of course, I still get session limited, but it's rarer now, and the LLM can see the full picture of my code instead of incremental hits to maintain consistency.

I hit the weekly limit last night. I have to wait till Sunday night to use it again, so I figured I would go back to my old system. Then, the most baffling thing popped up on the Claude log-in page.

*"Free Pro plan for users signing up with work email."*

Just. When. I. Paid. 20. United. States. Dollars. I. Could. Have. Gotten. It. For. Free.

Life has a cruel way of making one feel like a total idiot and money-waster. It's just like my forays into cryptocurrency when I buy coins and it suddenly plummets, then I sell coins and the prices *skyrocket*.

I took the offer because I wouldn't be able to work until Sunday if I didn't. Now I have *2* Claude Pro accounts, and once my weekly limit resets for the account I paid for, what exactly am I gonna do with that many Claude Code accounts? Make stupid prompts to often hit the limit so I can feel like I'm using both?

Slowly, I'm learning how to break free from needing AI. I crave for the day I don't need AI to code anymore. My ancestors (earlier computer science graduates) didn't use AI, they used Google, StackOverflow, and Reddit then got berated for their stupid questions. Cavemen (pre-internet programmers) used documentation books. I can do this.

I got my first paycheck on the 15th. It was quite unexpected because I didn't know salaries comes twice a month. I was beyond ecstatic when it did, though. My parents were concerned becaise they thought I wasn't actually working or I might not get paid. When the first splash of money hit my bank account, I felt vindicated and they were very happy. I treated my family lunch, not feeling fear about purchases anymore. Having money is nice, I missed the feeling. 

The change of being a responsible adult with money is indeed a scary and thrilling change. I still don't know what to do with my money, if I'm being honest. That's a privilege not a lot of people have, and I'm aware that not everyone can say that, but I don't want to spend too much or invest on high-yield stocks because what if I lose everything?

A lot of changes happened this month, but I want to talk about a truly horrific experience I would only wish on the people who did it to me.

Internet privacy and cybersecurity are important topics in the world of computer and information technology. For the longest time, my parents have warned me against computer cameras, checking what I post, and generally interacting with the online space. I'm a computer science graduate so I have secured my passwords and pictures for a long time. I only shared sensitive things to people I trust. 

But what happens when the very person you trusted with your whole life (at the time) is the one who decided to dox you?

I was doxed and harassed by someone I used to trust and love and the person they replaced me with. My private (not explicit, thankfully I had none) photos were shared on profile that impersonated me with my real name and phone number. My photos, name, and number was shared on explicit subreddits and Telegram channels, soliciting inappropriate messages and calls. They added and contacted my family and friends to make sure what they were doing is visible to me. They even emailed my boss, which was especially heartbreaking since I *just started this job*. I received email and text messages from the couple (using their full names!) who have been harassing me with threats toward my family. The worst part is that I can't fight back and these people still believe they are law-abiding citizens and are good and God-fearing. They justified their actions to anyone who confronted them for their vileness, blind to the fact that they are taking whatever argument prompted this attack too far.

They weren't smart about it, too. The emails I received were sent from an email address bearing their unique name. No one else in the world had access to the photos they shared. If you're gonna dox me, don't make it easy for me to tell people it's you.

All I could do was report the content they made to the platforms, even though content moderation on TikTok, Instagram, and Reddit are slow because of the sheer amount of reports they have to go through. The two of them are from Malaysia so I proceeded to report the accounts they made to contentforum.my and aduan.mcmc.gov.my. The posts were removed, but the TikTok account (the only one left) wasn't, which annoyed me because with the account still up and they still use it to impersonate me.

Because I gave them so little attention, they eventually stopped. I deactivated all my accounts for most of the month so they couldn't even reach out to me, and I'm just working hard and getting paid for it.

What's the latest update with them, you ask? They broke up.

They've blocked each other on TikTok, she removed his pictures from her account, and he has his Instagram deactivated. Yet, somehow, the impersonation account is still up. Hating me glued them for long enough to create the account, wouldn't it be decency to delete it? I feel like a child of divorce and the impersonating account is just left in the abyss.

I would share the [link to the account](tiktok.com/@marianneedic_) and ask you to report it, but as I'm writing this blog, there were some updates to the account.

The profile picture changed, it's now a tan-skinned man who's putting his middle finger up to the camera. The follower and the likes all turned to 0, meaning she deleted the posts permanently. It still uses my name, though! In the reposts are a lot of sad breakup posts. This is leaving me perplexed. Is she trying to make sure her sad posts are visible to the guy she *just* broke up with using the proof of her psychosis? Is she posting as *ME* or as *HERSELF*??? Is she saying that *I'M* the one who needs to let him go or is that *HER* thoughts? My mom read it as her impersonating me, but I don't want him, take him, please! Don't put your thoughts in my mouth!

This whole ordeal made me realize the importance of keeping your online persona and accounts secure, even with people we personally know. Love dies, people have the capability for evil, and sometimes, the people we trust the most are the ones who will twist the knife deepest.

Making sure your information is secure and untraceable is very important to keep ourselves safe. As an Application Developer, I still have a lot to learn about security. I secured my project, but I also need to secure my online identity. Is my secret online persona even a secret at this point?
