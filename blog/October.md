I've been planning to write a blog for a while. Actually, I attempted to write a progress documentation back in the first half of 2024, but that just turned into a confession to one of my classmates.

Luckily, I no longer have classmates to confess to.

I'm inspired by one of my friends who blogs a month of his life, a format I will be directly ripping off.

In the Philippines, October is considered a normal month. For most of the United States, I have been made aware that is not the case. Our spooky month is November, all the horror moview come out on November 1, after all. But for me, October 2025 is the single scariest month of my life.

Why? Because of a fundamental change in my life.

I started my first job on October 1. I officially have the job title of Application Developer, I officially have coworkers, I officially have a boss, and I officially have a salary. It's a lot to process.

When I first got my project, I was immediately overwhelmed. I thought I knew what I was getting into, but the realization of my underpreparedness sunk deep into my bones the second things were explained to me. My first day, I just stared at my computer with my mouth gaping as I ran the project documents through Claude to explain things to me clearly.

Did I understand things more or did I just feel relieved that I can have someone to rely on? Who's to say?

But I slowly chipped away at the tasks with the guidance of my project manager. Emphasis on slowly because apparently, I'm a human turtle. Why am I a human turtle? Because coding (without paying) using LLMs SUCK.

Only Claude is decent enough to code with and is what I exclusively use for code generation now, but with the free version, you only get a little use out of each and everyone of them before I hit the session limit and I have to wait 5 hours. And sometimes (too often), it generates garbage that is cut off, unusable, or I can't even access what they changed because it ended up in a random place only the LLM can access.

My solution, at first, was to use different accounts. I have FIVE EMAIL ACCOUNTS and I used all of them. I'll solve a problem in a page, then my session will end, so I'll go to another account and put all the files in there again, then I'll ask the next problem until I hit the session limit. 

That worked for a while, but then it was as if I hit a giant boulder while using my electric golfcart and it crushed me. 

When I pushed my project to a different environment, it took me almost a whole week to get it to work properly. I was pulling my hair out, I felt like I was shaking in my seat, my face was falling off my head. It wasn't a fun time.

The AI-generated code that were made in different Claude accounts had their routing hardcoded into the files, making my efforts to change the IP address and their ports moot. I didn't realize this until I read the code because the whole time I was just sending every file I can think of to Claude. Once I figured out (manually) what had happened, I fixed it relatively quickly and I felt better, but it was still a massive waste. I spent all day crying and restarting the environment when I could have just read the code and fixed it myself.

Now, I'm paying for Claude Code. Even though each month costs two days of work, at least I can finish two days of work in a day. Of course, I still get session limited, but it's rarer now, and the LLM can see the full picture of my code instead of incremental hits to maintain consistency. Slowly, I'm learning how to break free from needing it. I've been studying React and Node FOR THE FIRST TIME IN MY LIFE and I can make small edits for some witing logic. I crave for the day I don't need AI to code anymore.

I got my first paycheck on the 15th. It was quite unexpected because I didn't know salaries comes twice a month. I was beyond ecstatic when it did, though. My parents were concerned becaise they thought I wasn't actually working or I might not get paid. When the first splash of money hit my bank account, I felt vindicated and they were very happy. I treated my family lunch, not feeling fear about purchases anymore. Having money is nice, I missed the feeling. 

The change of being a responsible adult with money is indeed a scary and thrilling change. I still don't know what to do with my money, if I'm being honest. That's a privilege not a lot of people have, and I'm aware that not everyone can say that, but I don't want to spend too much or invest on high-yield stocks because what if I lose everything? But that's not why I called October the scariest month of my life.

Internet privacy and cybersecurity are important topics in the world of computer and information technology. For the longest time, my parents have warned me against computer cameras, checking what I post, and generally interacting with the online space. I'm a computer science graduate so I have secured my passwords and pictures for a long time. I only shared sensitive things to people I trust. 

But what happens when the very person you trusted with your whole life (at the time) is the one who decided to dox you?

This whole ordeal made me realize the importance of keeping your online persona and accounts secure, even with people we personally know. Love dies, people have the capability for evil, and sometimes, the people we trust the most are the ones who will twist the knife deepest.

Making sure your information is secure and untraceable is very important to keep ourselves safe. As an Application Developer, I still have a lot to learn about security. 

My project now is to make sure the websites I make are secure and impenetrable. With my terrifying experience, I need to make sure that my applications are safe for the business and the users.
